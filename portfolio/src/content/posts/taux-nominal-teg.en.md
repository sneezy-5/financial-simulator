When comparing two loan offers, the reflex is to look at the **nominal rate** —
the one shown in large type. It is not enough: two loans at the same nominal rate
can cost very different amounts.

## The nominal rate

This is the rate used to compute **interest** on the outstanding principal. It
drives the interest share of each instalment, but ignores everything else:
arrangement fees, borrower insurance, guarantees, commissions.

## The APR (effective global rate)

The **APR** rolls *all* mandatory costs of the loan into a single annual
percentage, comparable across offers. It is the only figure that answers: "for
the same service, which one costs me the least?"

A loan at a 8% nominal rate with heavy fees and expensive insurance can have a
higher APR than one advertised at 9% with no fees.

## What to check

- **The term**: a longer term lowers the instalment but raises the total cost —
  more months of interest.
- **The total cost**: principal + interest + fees + insurance over the full
  term. That is the amount actually paid out.
- **The amortization schedule**: early on, the instalment mostly pays interest;
  principal only really drops later.

A simulator that shows the instalment, total cost, interest share and
amortization schedule lets you compare two offers on the right basis — not on the
most visible number.
