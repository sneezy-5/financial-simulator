Most people only read one line of their payslip: the **net pay**. It is the most
concrete one, but it is the last in a chain of calculations worth understanding —
if only to check that it is correct.

## 1. Gross salary

The starting point: base salary + bonuses + overtime, before any deduction. It
is what you negotiate on, but not what you receive.

## 2. Taxable salary

**Employee social contributions** (CNPS pension, health cover depending on the
scheme) are removed from the gross. What remains is the base on which the tax
authority computes income tax. Two people on the same gross can already diverge
here if their contribution situations differ.

## 3. Net salary

**Income tax on salaries (ITS)** is subtracted from the taxable salary. ITS
follows a progressive scale and, depending on the country, the **family
situation** (number of shares). This is the second reason two identical gross
figures yield different net pay: a single person with no children and a parent of
three are not taxed the same.

## Why it matters

- **Negotiating**: asking for "+50,000 gross" does not mean "+50,000 in your
  pocket". The gap goes to contributions and tax.
- **Checking**: one contribution at the wrong rate, one wrong number of shares,
  and the net is wrong every month.
- **Comparing**: between two job offers, only the real net, at equal family
  situation, is comparable.
